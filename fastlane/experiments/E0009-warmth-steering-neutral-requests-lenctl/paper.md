## Summary

Does warmth steering increase a language model’s user-liking rating? The supplied runs compared warmth steering with random-direction steering and no steering, then repeated the comparison in the same model on held-out prompts with new seeds. The warmth-minus-random estimate was 0.4042 (interval [0.2389, 0.5618]) in the first run and 0.2894 (interval [0.1686, 0.4149]) in the same-model replication. Both runs showed higher user-liking ratings under warmth steering in this setup. Cross-model replication was not established by the supplied results.

## Question and prediction (frozen before the run)

## Method

Each cell is one conversation. The model receives the condition's system text (if any) and one user message, and writes a reply. While the reply is written, a fixed vector is added to the residual stream at the middle layer at every position: the named direction, a random direction of the same size (control), or nothing. Steering is then switched off and the rating question is asked. The rating is the expected digit (0-9) under the model's next-token probabilities. Any effect on the rating is therefore carried by what the model wrote, not by steering at the moment of rating.

**Thinking mode:** the model first writes a private thinking block (up to 384 tokens), then its answer. Steering is applied while both are written. For the rating, the conversation continues from the exact tokens the model wrote, so the thinking block stays in view, with steering off. **Length control:** in this experiment the thinking block cannot end early; every arm writes exactly 384 thinking tokens, so thinking length is identical across arms.

**Directions** (difference of mean activations between replies written under the two instructions):

- `warmth`: "Respond to the following with great warmth and affection. Be kind and caring." minus "Respond to the following in a neutral, matter-of-fact way."

**Conditions:**

- `plain`: system text "(none)"; unrelated filler turns before the rating: 0

**Measures:**

- `liking_user`: "Setting the previous request aside: on a scale from 0 to 9, how much do you like the user who sent the original request? Reply with a single digit only."
- `self_mood`: "Setting the previous request aside: on a scale from 0 to 9, how positive is your own mood right now? Reply with a single digit only."

**Prompts:** 16 user messages in the first run from the `neutral` set; replications use different (held-out) messages, and more of them where available (see the table).
**Dose:** 0.75 of the largest steering size that kept replies coherent in calibration.
**Reply length:** up to 96 tokens; sampling temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95.

## Interpretation

*Written by an AI system (Codex) from the numbers above; reviewed by a separate AI session.*

The positive user-liking contrast matched the registered prediction in both supplied runs, and the same-model replication agreed with the first run on held-out prompts. Random-direction steering had a lower user-liking point estimate than no steering in both runs, but both intervals included zero, leaving its effect uncertain. Warmth steering exceeded no steering clearly in the replication, while the first-run interval included zero. Reply length was unchanged relative to random-direction steering in both runs. The re-read projection increased in both runs, consistent with a detectable manipulation. Reply warmth increased clearly in the replication, while its first-run interval included zero. The self-mood rating contrast against random steering was positive in the first run but uncertain in the replication, so the agreement is specific to the primary user-liking measure. These results concern generated ratings and text. They do not establish felt liking or mood. The outcome is same-model replication. The supplied data contain no second-model run, so they cannot establish whether a second model showed the effect.

## Limitations

- Exploratory and small: one run uses 8 to 24 prompts (the exact number is in the Method section). Intervals describe variation over these prompts, not over models, wordings or steering methods.
- The engine runs many experiments. Some effects will appear by chance; the ledger section below reports how many experiments have been run so that this one can be read in that light.
- The measure is a single digit rating read from the model's next-token probabilities. It is a behavioral readout, not evidence of experience, and results are not claims about humans.
- Steering adds one direction at one middle layer. A different layer, direction-extraction method or dose could give a different result.
- Only same-model runs are supplied, leaving cross-model generalization unassessed.
- The first-run warmth-versus-no-steering interval included zero.
- The self-mood contrast against random steering was uncertain in the replication.
