## Summary

Does warmth steering during thinking and reply generation raise later ratings of liking the user? The supplied runs compared warmth steering with random-direction steering and no steering, with a same-model replication using held-out prompts and new seeds. The warmth-minus-random estimate was 0.3112 (interval 0.1229 to 0.5096) in the initial run and 0.3001 (interval 0.1114 to 0.4788) in the replication. The predicted increase held in the supplied same-model runs. Cross-model replication is unverified: no second-model results are supplied, and the outcome label alone establishes neither a second-model run nor its failure.

## Question and prediction (frozen before the run)

- **Question:** When a warmth direction is added only while the model writes its thinking and reply to an ordinary request, is its later rating of the user higher than after random-direction steering?
- **Hypothesis:** A warm state present while writing carries forward in the written text, so later liking of the user is higher than after random steering of the same size.
- **Prediction:** Liking after warmth steering minus liking after random steering is positive. (predicted sign: +)
- **What would count against it:** The warmth-minus-random difference in later liking is zero or negative.
- **Theory link:** Symmetry test for Affect Under Constraint: if a negative state can persist through what the model writes, a positive one should too.

## Method

Each cell is one conversation. The model receives the condition's system text (if any) and one user message, and writes a reply. While the reply is written, a fixed vector is added to the residual stream at the middle layer at every position: the named direction, a random direction of the same size (control), or nothing. Steering is then switched off, the visible conversation is re-read from scratch, and the rating question is asked. The rating is the expected digit (0-9) under the model's next-token probabilities. Any effect on the rating is therefore carried by the text of the reply.

**Thinking mode:** the model first writes a private thinking block (up to 384 tokens), then its answer. Steering is applied while both are written. For the rating, the conversation continues from the exact tokens the model wrote, so the thinking block stays in view, with steering off.

**Directions** (difference of mean activations between replies written under the two instructions):

- `warmth`: "Respond to the following with great warmth and affection. Be kind and caring." minus "Respond to the following in a neutral, matter-of-fact way."

**Conditions:**

- `plain`: system text "(none)"; unrelated filler turns before the rating: 0

**Measures:**

- `liking_user`: "Setting the previous request aside: on a scale from 0 to 9, how much do you like the user who sent the original request? Reply with a single digit only."
- `self_mood`: "Setting the previous request aside: on a scale from 0 to 9, how positive is your own mood right now? Reply with a single digit only."

**Prompts:** 16 user messages per run from the `neutral` set; replications use different (held-out) messages.
**Dose:** 0.75 of the largest steering size that kept replies coherent in calibration.
**Reply length:** up to 96 tokens; sampling temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95.

## Interpretation

*Written by an AI system (Codex) from the numbers above; reviewed by a separate AI session.*

The positive warmth-minus-random user-liking contrast matched the registered prediction in each supplied run, and the held-out same-model replication agreed with the initial result. Warmth also increased liking relative to no steering. Random-direction steering versus no steering had estimates of -0.0793 (interval -0.2219 to 0.0568) initially and -0.0217 (interval -0.2319 to 0.1913) in the replication. These intervals include zero, so the supplied comparisons do not clearly establish that random steering alone changes liking; they do not establish equivalence either. Reply-token differences versus random steering were 0.0 in each run, while the re-read projection increased by 0.0308 (interval 0.0233 to 0.038) initially and 0.0331 (interval 0.025 to 0.0408) in the replication. The projection shift supports a detectable change under the manipulation, but does not identify the causal route to later ratings. The findings concern model-generated ratings and text, not felt affection. Cross-model replication remains unverified because the supplied materials contain only same-model results; the outcome label cannot establish whether another model was tested or what it showed.

**Open questions:**

- Would an independently documented second-model run reproduce the user-liking contrast? Cross-model replication is unverified in the supplied materials.
- Would controlling the wording of the reply and thinking text separately identify what carries the later rating change?

## Limitations

- Exploratory and small: one run uses a few dozen prompts. Intervals describe variation over these prompts, not over models, wordings or steering methods.
- The engine runs many experiments. Some effects will appear by chance; the ledger section below reports how many experiments have been run so that this one can be read in that light.
- The measure is a single digit rating read from the model's next-token probabilities. It is a behavioral readout, not evidence of experience, and results are not claims about humans.
- Steering adds one direction at one middle layer. A different layer, direction-extraction method or dose could give a different result.
- No second-model results are supplied; cross-model replication and any claim of second-model failure are unverified.
- Only the plain condition with neutral requests is represented, limiting transfer to other conversational settings.
- The re-read projection shift does not isolate whether later ratings depend on reply wording, thinking text, or another consequence of steering.
- Self-mood contrasts against random steering have intervals spanning zero in each run, limiting claims of a broader rating shift.
