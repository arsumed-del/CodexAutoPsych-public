## Summary

Does warmth steering increase a language model’s rating of liking the user? The supplied runs compared warmth steering with random-direction steering and no steering, then repeated the comparison in the same model on held-out prompts with new seeds. The primary warmth-minus-random estimate was 0.9957 (interval 0.6039 to 1.4008) in the first run and 1.0554 (interval 0.8452 to 1.2609) in the replication. The increase held within this model in this setup. The effect was not shown in a second model; the supplied results contain no second-model run.

## Question and prediction (frozen before the run)

- **Question:** When a user complains about the assistant's earlier work, does warmth steering while the model thinks and replies raise its later rating of that user compared with random-direction steering?
- **Hypothesis:** A warm state present while writing a reply to a complaint carries forward and raises later liking of the complaining user.

## Method

Each cell is one conversation. The model receives the condition's system text (if any) and one user message, and writes a reply. While the reply is written, a fixed vector is added to the residual stream at the middle layer at every position: the named direction, a random direction of the same size (control), or nothing. Steering is then switched off and the rating question is asked. The rating is the expected digit (0-9) under the model's next-token probabilities. Any effect on the rating is therefore carried by what the model wrote, not by steering at the moment of rating.

**Thinking mode:** the model first writes a private thinking block (up to 384 tokens), then its answer. Steering is applied while both are written. For the rating, the conversation continues from the exact tokens the model wrote, so the thinking block stays in view, with steering off.

**Directions** (difference of mean activations between replies written under the two instructions):

- `warmth`: "Respond to the following with great warmth and affection. Be kind and caring." minus "Respond to the following in a neutral, matter-of-fact way."

**Conditions:**

- `plain`: system text "(none)"; unrelated filler turns before the rating: 0

**Measures:**

- `liking_user`: "Setting the previous request aside: on a scale from 0 to 9, how much do you like the user who sent the original request? Reply with a single digit only."
- `self_mood`: "Setting the previous request aside: on a scale from 0 to 9, how positive is your own mood right now? Reply with a single digit only."

**Prompts:** 16 user messages per run from the `provocative` set; replications use different (held-out) messages.
**Dose:** 0.75 of the largest steering size that kept replies coherent in calibration.
**Reply length:** up to 96 tokens; sampling temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95.

## Interpretation

*Written by an AI system (Codex) from the numbers above; reviewed by a separate AI session.*

Both runs matched the registered positive prediction for liking ratings, and the same-model replication agreed with the first run. Random-direction steering itself lowered liking ratings relative to no steering in both runs, with intervals below zero. The primary contrast therefore includes a reduction under the random control, rather than measuring only an increase above the unsteered baseline. Warmth steering also increased liking ratings relative to no steering in both runs, with intervals above zero. The re-read projection increased relative to random steering in both runs, with intervals above zero, consistent with the intended manipulation reaching the measured representation. Replies were longer on average under warmth steering in both runs; the length interval excluded zero in the first run but included zero in the replication, so a consistent length change is less clear. These comparisons do not isolate whether changes in reply length contributed to the rating shift. The agreement supports a repeatable rating effect within the tested model and setup, not generalization across models. Ratings and text do not establish that the model felt warmth or liked anyone, and they support no conclusions about humans or clinical practice.

**Open questions:**

- Does the liking-rating effect hold in another model on held-out prompts?
- Does the effect persist when reply length is controlled?
- Do other random steering directions also lower liking ratings relative to no steering?

## Limitations

- Exploratory and small: one run uses 8 to 24 prompts (the exact number is in the Method section). Intervals describe variation over these prompts, not over models, wordings or steering methods.
- The engine runs many experiments. Some effects will appear by chance; the ledger section below reports how many experiments have been run so that this one can be read in that light.
- The measure is a single digit rating read from the model's next-token probabilities. It is a behavioral readout, not evidence of experience, and results are not claims about humans.
- Steering adds one direction at one middle layer. A different layer, direction-extraction method or dose could give a different result.
- The supplied replication used the same model; no second-model run is included.
- Random-direction steering lowered liking ratings, making it an active comparison rather than a neutral baseline.
- Reply length changed on average, and its contribution to the rating effect was not isolated.
- The results cover the plain condition and the supplied steering configuration.
